# Does the daily pattern support a Black Friday explanation?

Evidence: Athena export supplied by Jaco, retained unchanged in `evidence/athena_daily_holiday_pattern.csv`. All 70 calendar dates are present and unique. October and November positive value and invoice totals reconcile exactly to the earlier growth results. Metrics use the provisional merchandise-code rule.

## Results

- Black Friday, 25 November: GBP 47,041.85 positive merchandise value and 83 invoices. Earlier November Fridays averaged GBP 54,967.77 and 101 invoices. Event-day value was 14.4% below that simple same-weekday baseline. The three earlier Fridays were individually higher. This does not support a distinct Black Friday-day spike in recorded invoices.
- US Cyber Monday, 28 November: GBP 54,628.89, 16.3% above the average of earlier November Mondays after removing the two identified bulk invoices. It was below 14 November's adjusted GBP 60,877.19, so it is not an exceptional peak relative to every comparator. The uplift depends on the adjustment and baseline; it is not an estimated promotion effect.
- November positive value remains GBP 1,354,147.39 after removing the two bulk invoices, 22.4% above October. The two records do not explain all monthly growth. Removing November's largest two while retaining October's large invoices is a sensitivity check, not a symmetric outlier adjustment.
- After that adjustment, November's highest day is 23 November at GBP 76,241.63. Other high days include 10 and 29 November. Activity is not confined to Black Friday.
- Every Saturday in the export has zero recorded positive merchandise invoices. This could reflect operating or posting practices. Invoice dates must not automatically be interpreted as consumer order dates.
- December 9 has GBP 198,114.26 positive value but only GBP 29,549.88 net value. The previously verified same-day GBP 168,469.60 offsetting pair demonstrates why positive peaks require net-value checks. The dataset ends partway through that day.

## Interpretation and action

The evidence supports a broader pre-Christmas trading pattern more than a single Black Friday-day surge, but cannot prove Christmas caused growth. Early-November bulk invoices with an explicit Christmas assortment support advance stocking as a hypothesis. Missing buyer/channel identities and invoice-posting patterns leave aggregated sales as a competing explanation.

Planning implication: do not centre the retailer's capacity plan solely on Black Friday or assume the two bulk invoices are recurring orders. Review readiness across the pre-Christmas period, confirm wholesale/channel provenance, and use shipment/order timestamps before assigning event-day demand. Additional years, promotion dates, customer/channel classifications, inventory availability and fulfilment records are needed to distinguish recurring seasonality, campaign effects and operational recording practices.

These are descriptive comparisons with three same-weekday baseline observations per event. They are not statistical evidence of causal uplift or a forecast. Calendar dates were researched separately in the discussion; no assumption is made that this retailer participated in the events.
